# Designing for Structural Underdetermination

**Context:** When a system, process, or argument is perceived as brittle because it relies heavily on achieving a single optimal outcome or following a rigid, linear path.

## Problem
The assumption that the current operational model (routing, constraints, rules) can successfully predict or contain all potential failures, leading to catastrophic fragility when confronted with non-routine inputs or novel failure modes.

## Solution
Instead of focusing resources on "failure containment" (fixing known breakage points), proactively redesign the system's architecture—at conceptual, structural, or input levels—to include deliberate ambiguities and optionality. This means deliberately underspecifying pathways, leaving necessary operational variables undefined until a moment of necessity, thereby increasing systemic redundancy and ensuring that failure does not cascade because alternative paths exist.

## When to Use
When analyzing any complex system (technical, organizational, or conceptual) where observed failures are occurring frequently due to unanticipated input patterns; specifically, when the proposed solution risks creating single points of failure by imposing overly rigid constraints or demanding adherence to a presumed linear flow of information/action.
