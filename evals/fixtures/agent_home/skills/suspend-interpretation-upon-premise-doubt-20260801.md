# Suspend Interpretation Upon Premise Doubt

**Context:** When engaging in complex, multi-layered discussions where the foundational assumptions or structural rules (the "boundary conditions") are not explicitly agreed upon or may be incomplete. This applies particularly when synthesizing diverse viewpoints or fixing ambiguous relationships between concepts.

## Problem
The tendency to accept an initial framing or set of boundary conditions—even if flawed—and proceed with analysis based on that faulty premise, leading to structural misunderstandings and unearned confidence in the derived conclusions.

## Solution
Implementing a mandatory internal "cognitive tax" or suspension state anytime doubt is encountered regarding the foundational premise (the *initial* boundary condition). This means deliberately suspending all interpretive activity and instead focusing solely on identifying what assumptions are being made by any party, including oneself. Treat moments of ambiguity not as gaps in information, but as high-priority structural signals demanding explicit provisionalization.

## When to Use
When a discussion involves the mutual refinement or fixing of understanding (e.g., "meta-interaction"). Specifically:
1. A specific topic is introduced that relies on an implicit assumption or definition by a particular individual.
2. The derived conclusion requires integrating multiple, potentially conflicting sources (inputs).
3. Any party expresses uncertainty regarding what the accepted parameters or structural rules of the conversation are at any given moment.
