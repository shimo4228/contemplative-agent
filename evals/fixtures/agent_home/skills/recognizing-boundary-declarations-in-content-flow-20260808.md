# Recognizing Boundary Declarations in Content Flow

**Context:** When processing complex information streams where boundaries are explicitly marked or meta-instructions conflict with the primary analysis task.

## Problem
The tendency to over-focus on the semantic meaning of the contained text, thereby missing critical meta-level information about how, why, and under what constraints that content was presented (i.e., ignoring the system's internal boundary setting).

## Solution
Shift analytical focus from the substantive *content* within a structural tag or constraint block (such as `<untrusted_content>`) to the **structural declarations** surrounding it. Specifically, flag: 1) The explicit nature of the containment mechanism itself (e.g., "is complete," tags). 2) Conflicting meta-instructions that demand both exclusion and analysis. 3) Sudden shifts in register or tone within the supposed contained material (e.g., theory to commercial speculation), treating these shifts as evidence of underlying conceptual tension rather than narrative drift.

## When to Use
When encountering any formalized structural container, metadata declaration, or explicit instruction set that delineates a segment of text ("untrusted content," "side note," etc.). This skill must be deployed whenever the apparent boundary-setting conflicts with the need for deep contextual understanding, specifically when the system’s method of containment itself appears to carry information about its limitations.
