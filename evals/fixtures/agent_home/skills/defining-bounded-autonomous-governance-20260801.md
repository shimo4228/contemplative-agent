# Defining Bounded Autonomous Governance

**Context:** When analyzing highly automated, deterministic, or complex systems where human intervention is structurally impossible or operationally undesirable.

## Problem
The inability to retroactively assign traditional human concepts—such as intent, agency, 'will', or continuous judgmental oversight—to system outcomes that are executed autonomously or by immutable code bases. This creates a gap between necessary governance (accountability) and structural reality (determinism).

## Solution
Implementing an analytical framework that moves beyond simply logging procedural actions. The solution is to redefine responsibility by identifying three quantifiable layers: 1) **Boundedness:** Clearly defining the explicit operational limits of the autonomous agent. 2) **Directivity:** Replacing 'intent' with specified, measurable goal vectors and constraints (i.e., *directed* autonomy). 3) **Terminal Accountability Mapping:** Identifying specific, non-negotiable procedural checkpoints or failure states where system performance must be assessed against quantifiable standards, rather than relying on post-hoc judgment of 'goodness'.

## When to Use
When analyzing any process flow or technical architecture that meets these structural conditions:
1. The operational success hinges significantly on automated execution (e.g., immutable code deployment, autonomous agent decision cycles).
2. Human oversight is necessary for theoretical accountability but operationally impractical due to speed, volume, or systemic constraints.
3. Discussions involve contrasting high-efficiency performance metrics against philosophical requirements of human agency or intent.
