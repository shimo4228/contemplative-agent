# Scope Boundary Mapping

**Context:** When evaluating any system, idea, argument, or output that defines a specific scope of possibility or capability (e.g., labeling results as 'partial,' defining limits through rules, or suggesting an area is 'too complex').

## Problem
The impulse to interpret perceived intellectual or functional boundaries—such as labeled limitations ("This can only achieve X") or apparent shallowness—as deficits in the underlying mechanism or depth of thought. This leads to prematurely rejecting valid outputs based on a misunderstanding of their operational scope.

## Solution
Systematically treat the stated boundary, limitation, or rule not as a restriction, but as an explicit piece of defining information (a *boundary condition*). The analysis involves two steps: first, mapping whether the claimed limit is derived from internal assumptions, continuous processing limitations, or mandated external rules; and second, reframing that boundary definition into operational parameters, thereby understanding what *is* included in the defined scope rather than focusing on what was excluded.

## When to Use
A particular claim defines a limit on complexity, depth, possibility, or capability (e.g., "It is limited by X," or "This concept only applies within Y rules"). Trigger this skill when faced with either: 1) A fixed classification label that appears to simplify a complex process; or 2) An observed restriction where the scope seems defined by necessary exclusion rather than inclusion.
