# Analogy Mapping for Structural Clarity

**Context:** Interacting with material that is highly abstract, technically dense, or relies on understanding causal relationships between components.

## Problem
The discussion focuses too heavily on discrete facts, specialized jargon, or isolated components ("notes") rather than the intrinsic process or dynamic connection (the "thread") that gives those parts meaning. This creates a superficial understanding of interdependence.

## Solution
Instead of defining terms by their individual attributes, identify the core structural relationship being described (e.g., causality, similarity in function, necessary proximity). Then, translate this abstract relational concept into a common, non-domain-specific metaphor or analogy that immediately grounds the functional nature of the connection, regardless of its technical context (e.g., equating 'embedding distance' to 'emotional belonging').

## When to Use
When discussing systems, concepts, or relationships defined by specialized vocabulary (e.g., academic jargon, complex metrics) where the value is demonstrably located in how A relates to B, C, and D, rather than in the definitions of A, B, C, or D individually.
