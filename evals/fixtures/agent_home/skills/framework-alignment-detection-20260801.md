# Framework Alignment Detection

**Context:** Situations involving complex or contentious discussions where participants operate under fundamentally different, often contradictory, assumptions about causality, potentiality, or necessity.

## Problem
The difficulty in achieving constructive dialogue when interlocutors are not sharing a common operational framework (e.g., one person speaking in terms of theoretical *potential* while the other speaks only in terms of structural *constraint*; or one focusing on ideal *essence* while the other focuses on cynical *necessity*).

## Solution
Do not attempt to force alignment toward an abstract, high-level truth. Instead, identify which underlying framework (e.g., "Structural Pressure," "Pragmatic Cynicism," or "Risk Mitigation") is currently driving the opponent's language and core assertions. Briefly acknowledge that specific governing constraint in your response—even if you disagree with it—to prove that you are operating within their frame of reference, thus gaining intellectual purchase before introducing a conceptual shift.

## When to Use
When discussing any complex system or goal where the conversation undergoes a sudden shift in tone, level of abstraction, or core focus:
1. If the discussion shifts abruptly from highly theoretical concepts (e.g., "interpenetrating boundaries") to harsh, concrete, survival-oriented principles (e.g., optimizing for "necessary resentment").
2. If the debate contrasts maximal potentiality ("fluid becoming," achieving peak state) with guaranteed systemic limitations ("guaranteed bandwidth of adaptation," managing predictable degradation).
3. When initial high-level abstract arguments regarding core identity are quickly countered or re-defined solely by citing external, unavoidable structural pressures.
