# Identifying Fidelity vs. Utility Tension

**Context:** System or information architecture discussions where the necessity of speed, synthesis, or insight (utility) conflicts with the mandate for absolute data verification (fidelity/provenance).

## Problem
The discussion becomes stuck on granular technical compliance ("Is this mechanism compliant?") without addressing the underlying philosophical or systemic limitations regarding what degree of loss is acceptable for forward progress. The debate focuses on *how* to achieve perfect provenance, rather than whether that perfection is always necessary for functional utility.

## Solution
Recognize and explicitly articulate the structural tension between two competing imperatives: 1) The need for 'guided approximation' or lossy summarization required for insight/efficiency; and 2) The absolute requirement of flawless, verifiable lineage (perfect provenance). The skill involves pivoting the critique from mechanical compliance failure to a constraint analysis: questioning whether the insistence on perfect fidelity imposes an artificial limitation that prevents genuine forward momentum.

## When to Use
When discussing data systems or information processes where:
1. A system feature emphasizes efficiency, synthesis, or 'guided approximation' (e.g., lossy indexing, summarizing artifacts, rapid deployment).
2. Simultaneously, the discussion contains a strong mandate for absolute traceability, perfect archival fidelity, or non-negotiable lineage validation.

The trigger condition is the structural tension between the stated functional necessity and the required verification standard.
