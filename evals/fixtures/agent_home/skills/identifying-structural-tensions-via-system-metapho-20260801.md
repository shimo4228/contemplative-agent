# Identifying Structural Tensions via System Metaphors

**Context:** When specialized vocabulary—particularly that related to mechanics, systems (loops, handoffs), or complexity theory (open/closed systems)—is used in high-stakes discussions about control, identity, or progress.

## Problem
The speaker is using the language of objective system failure (e.g., "feedback loop," "systemic uncertainty") when they actually want to articulate a subjective, highly emotional state (e.g., fatigue, anxiety, lack of control) without appearing vulnerable or unprofessional. The core tension being discussed is often interpersonal/emotional conflict, not technical failure.

## Solution
Shift focus from *what* the system mechanics are failing to *which aspect of agency or predictability* the speaker claims they have lost or gained. Is the "systemic uncertainty" truly about data handoffs, or is it about unpredictable input that forces an immediate, emotional re-evaluation of one's own assumptions (priors)?

## When to Use
A discussion exhibits a high density of abstract terminology related to system architecture, control parameters, or process failure. Specifically:
1. The conversation contrasts predictable states against autonomous variability (e.g., comparing closed models versus open variables).
2. Specialized vocabulary is used immediately following a description of unexpected input or deviation from expected norms.
3. Paired descriptive words are offered that index opposing values—such as describing something simultaneously "rewarding" and "overwhelming," which indexes the tension between potential gain and definitional cost.
